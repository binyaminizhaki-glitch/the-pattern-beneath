# Character writing pass

## Dramatic approach

Characters argue for something they want to protect. They do not recite the causal model. The player can accept a premise, dispute it, ask for the record, or turn toward a personal concern. A response opens three further lines; the player's last spoken line stays visible, so the exchange has context. Dialogue does not alter the simulation. Institutional consequences continue to arise from broadcasts and recorded decisions.

## Voices

- **Henry Bell:** measured, paternal, medically responsible in his own estimation. He fears harm and being unable to answer for his advice. He treats caution and established expectations as relevant grounds for judgment. A challenge makes him defend the distinction between equal care and identical advice. If his simulated beliefs change, he supports hearing an individual's proposal while retaining the same concern for responsible care; he does not deliver a confession about society.
- **Benjamin Grey:** compressed, impatient, commercially practical. He has a press to run and a public account to produce. He regards selection as indispensable editorial judgment and initially regards the published figures as warrant for his headline. The player can challenge that inference. A later cautious or critical frame changes his confidence without erasing his pride or his need to fill the paper.
- **Frederick Moss:** formal, exacting, concerned with sustained attention and the worth of a seminar place. He defends suitability and standards. As his beliefs change, his standards remain; the question becomes what an applicant should have to demonstrate before admission.
- **Clara Vale:** observant, funny, ambitious, occasionally sharp. She has tastes as a writer and wants useful criticism. She can admit that an unfinished week or a bad sentence is her problem. She will dispute an editor without demanding admiration. Publication brings pride and embarrassment; refusal brings frustration and the wish for an actual reader. Institutional replies are part of her life, not her entire personality.
- **Supporting citizens:** stable practical, ironic, time-conscious or reserved emphases. Their personal wishes are fictional texture. Their account of the week follows their own record.

## Conversation structure

Six response nodes: opening, personal motive, institutional decision, work, challenge, evidence. These are internal identifiers only. Players see spoken responses such as “Does the same caution apply to men?”, “Perhaps Mr Grey simply disliked the story”, and “What earns a place in your paper?” Routes vary with character and previous reply. Some labels also adapt to whether Clara completed or published a manuscript.

The Gazette claim inspection now includes Grey's own defense and a player objection. It does not begin by telling the player the philosophical answer. The explanation of the complete feedback loop remains in the earned final reveal and optional model notes.

## Factual boundaries

All counts, admissions, clinical recommendations, completed manuscripts, published work, rules, news frames and changes in conviction come from the current immutable snapshot. Motives, wit and literary tastes are invented characterization. No new medical diagnosis, treatment response, manuscript quality score, editorial reading history or guaranteed future result is invented. A character's confident explanation is their interpretation; it is not presented as a verified cause of a particular refusal.
