# Research and literary boundaries

Primary text: Charlotte Perkins Gilman, The Yellow Wall-Paper (1892), pp. 647–656, [NLM facsimile](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/exhibition/theliteratureofprescription/exhibitionAssets/digitalDocs/The-Yellow-Wall-Paper.pdf). The opening presents John as both husband and physician; restrictions on work and secret writing make authority material. The narrator is already distressed. The game neither diagnoses her nor claims her symptoms were wholly socially manufactured.

Two brief fragments used in the game: “But I don't care—there is something strange about the house—I can feel it.” is deliberately NOT used because of transcription punctuation differences. Verified fragments used instead: “Personally, I disagree with their ideas.” (p. 648) and “he hates to have me write a word.” (p. 649). Total quoted source text is under 25 words.

[NLM contextual exhibit](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/exhibition/theliteratureofprescription/exhibition0.html) and [context](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/exhibition/theliteratureofprescription/exhibition3.html). [Gilman's 1913 retrospective, reproduced by Yale](https://teachersinstitute.yale.edu/curriculum/units/1999/1/99.01.07/5), contextualizes intellectual restriction; biography and fiction remain distinct.

[Berger excerpt](https://people.wku.edu/steve.groce/thesacredcanopy-berger.pdf): nomos concerns an established order of meaning, represented here through beliefs, rules and interpretations together. [Berkeley social cognition discussion](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/SocialCognitionWeb/CogPerspect/CogPerspect_supp.htm): interpretations affect conduct; self-fulfilling prophecy adds an apparently confirming feedback loop. The Thomas theorem is broader than this narrower mechanism.

The organic wallpaper is an interpretive visual motif, not a claim to decode the story's ambiguity. All coefficients and population controls are illustrative, not empirical findings.
