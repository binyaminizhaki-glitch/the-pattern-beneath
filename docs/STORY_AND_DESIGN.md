# Story and design direction — 5 November 2026

Start with the room. The narrator wants work, company and a say in her own day. John treats her view as less reliable than his. Because he is her husband and doctor, his account becomes a room, a schedule and a ban on work. Writing has to be hidden. The wallpaper becomes something she can attend to, then something she thinks she understands. By the end she speaks as the woman who has got out of it. The breakdown happens in the fiction; it is not merely an insulting label.

“They tell her she is going mad, so she does” is useful shorthand but not the book’s exact sequence. John initially denies she is seriously ill and later insists she is improving. We do not invent a quotation in which he calls her mad. The sharper contradiction is that her growing withdrawal looks like successful care to him.

One reading connects this to nomos: an account becomes the accepted order, then sets the conditions people live under. Those conditions can help produce what the account appears to explain. This is an interpretation. The narrator is already unwell at the beginning.

## How it plays

Five short room readings sit before the town, after weeks 2, 4 and 6, and after the final comparison. The book has its own sequence; its summer is not the town’s eight-week clock.

1. Write a private line, ask for another room, look at the paper. Hide the writing to move on.
2. Ask to leave. John’s answer keeps her in the room.
3. Trace three parts of the pattern. A shape becomes a woman.
4. Look behind the bars. John’s account of improvement and her private certainty no longer match.
5. Pull three strips. Reveal the original ending illustration and her circling the room.

These are acts of reading a fixed story. There is no correct answer that cures her and no score for the breakdown. The verbs change from writing and asking to watching, hiding and pulling. The town is where different messages can change recorded outcomes.

Clara Vale is an invented writer. She is not Gilman’s unnamed narrator. Her dialogue uses actual access, manuscript and publication records. She has wants, humor and doubts, rather than a speech explaining the model. Publication totals do not imply her mental health.

## Words and visual form

Use plain English and concrete verbs. Henry fears doing harm, Grey has a paper to sell, Moss wants a serious class, Clara wants to write well. Shorten speeches while keeping those motives. Leave technical numbers in records where they can be inspected.

Keep the existing paintings. Remove extra texture from the controls, decorative all-cap labels and oversized dialogue. The room puts the image beside a reading page with three actions and a source passage. The wallpaper increasingly takes over the picture. Its last strips uncover the original illustration. Mobile gives the picture a short usable area and keeps the actions on screen.

## Model boundaries

The town’s equations, starting ability, keyed random draws, access checks and manuscript requirement are unchanged. Text describing output was simplified. The room has a separate pure reducer: objects and strips cannot advance a week or change town records. Replay clears reading state. The PDF and illustration are local assets; no backend or external runtime service was added.
